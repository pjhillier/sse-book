The Security-Engineering Convergence

How Systems Security Engineering Unifies Technology, Security, and Mission Outcomes

Peter J. Hillier, CD, CISSP

Under professional review — not yet published

A practical technical reference for the people who specify, engineer, acquire, assess, operate and lead complex systems.

The central argument

Security must be engineered into the capability

A system can satisfy its security controls and still fail its mission when assumptions about interfaces, authority, dependencies or operating conditions prove wrong.

The Security-Engineering Convergence examines Systems Security Engineering (SSE) as the security specialty integrated within systems engineering. It does not present SSE as a replacement for systems engineering, cybersecurity operations, risk management or compliance. Instead, it shows how those contributions can connect to the engineering work that shapes system behaviour across its life cycle.

The book follows a practical line of reasoning: establish the needed mission outcome, characterize the system and its context, investigate adversity and loss, develop requirements, choose and assess architectures, evaluate evidence, and maintain the resulting basis for trust through acquisition, operation, change and retirement.

Engineering confidence is earned through clear claims, justified design decisions and evidence whose limitations are understood.

Who the book is for

Systems engineers

Integrate protection needs, security-specific analyses and assurance into established engineering decisions and artefacts.

Cybersecurity practitioners

Connect controls, threat analysis, assessments and operations to requirements, architecture, verification and mission consequence.

Acquisition and leadership

Understand what to demand from suppliers, what evidence means, and where decision authority and residual uncertainty remain.

What makes it practical

Methods, examples and decision records

The manuscript uses scenarios and case records to demonstrate engineering reasoning rather than merely describe successful outcomes. It distinguishes hypothetical case assumptions, analytical models, executed teaching-code tests, technical evidence and the decisions that evidence may—or may not—support.

Traceable engineering

Mission needs, losses, requirements, architectures, evaluations and decisions remain connected.

Multiple system contexts

Worked material includes administrative authority, cyber-physical motion, information flow and trusted-source compromise.

Reusable practitioner tools

Worksheets support documenting protection needs, allocation, architectural trade-offs, evidence applicability and change impact.

Illustrative case material must not be mistaken for evidence that a corresponding real-world operational system has been tested, authorized or validated.

About the author

Peter J. Hillier, CD, CISSP

Peter J. Hillier is a Canadian cybersecurity consultant, Systems Security Engineering practitioner, and founder of Hillier Information Protection Solutions Inc.

With more than four decades of experience spanning military intelligence, national defence, enterprise cybersecurity, and security governance, Peter brings an operational and systems-level perspective to the engineering of trustworthy capabilities.

A 20-year veteran of the Canadian Armed Forces, his subsequent career has included enterprise Chief Information Security Officer roles, cybersecurity incident-response leadership, security architecture, and advisory work supporting complex defence systems and major naval acquisition programmes.

His more recent work focuses on Systems Security Engineering, cyber resilience, supply-chain assurance, secure procurement, and integrating security considerations into the engineering and sustainment of mission-critical capabilities.

A published practitioner, educator, and contributor to international security-engineering initiatives, Peter advocates moving beyond compliance as the primary measure of security toward disciplined engineering, meaningful evidence, and accountable decisions.

Through his writing and professional work, he seeks to make engineering-led security more accessible to the engineers, cybersecurity practitioners, acquisition professionals, and leaders responsible for the systems upon which modern institutions depend.
